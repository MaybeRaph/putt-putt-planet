## v1.8.2 — golden-hour lighting (done)
- **`rendering/lighting.ts`**: per-world golden-hour looks (`GOLDEN`: sky stops, warm fog, sun colour/strength, cool fill, warm bounce, IBL strength, sun elevation 14–20° and azimuth, shaft strength). `goldenTheme(theme)` restyles a course theme for rendering only (`PlayScene.loadHole`, `MenuBackdrop.show`); course data keeps its daytime colours, so other clients and the course cards are unaffected. Thick fog (the Fog Bank omen) keeps its grey. Space keeps its starlight, with a slightly lower star.
- **Moving sun**: `sunAt(kit, t)` swings the sun ±16° in compass and ±4° in height over a 5-minute cycle (about 0.25°/s). `Renderer.aimSun()` re-aims the light, shadow camera, sky disc and the shared `liveSun` vector (the water's highlight uniform holds that same vector) every frame. Shadows were already redrawn every frame, so it costs nothing. Setting `movingSun` (default on; off under reduced motion), which puts the sun back in its resting place.
- **Light shafts** (`rendering/GodRays.ts`): a ray march through a thin ground-hugging haze (height falloff 5 m, up to 48 m), sampling the sun's shadow map (`sampler2DShadow`) at each step, at half resolution (Medium 0.35) with interleaved-gradient jitter and a two-pass blur. Henyey–Greenstein forward scattering (g 0.62) makes it strongest looking into the sun; air in shadow dims the image slightly, which deepens the beams. Needs the scene depth: the multisampled scene target now carries a `DepthTexture`. Compositing happens inside the grade pass (no extra full-screen pass; doing it separately cost ~1 ms on High). Steps: Medium 12, High 20, Ultra 24; off on Low (no shadows).
- **Backlit foliage**: `addTranslucency()` patches three's light loop so grass blades, canopies and plants glow with the sun's light when it is behind them (respects shadows).
- **Sky**: soft-edged sun disc, tight corona and wide glow; warm horizon on the sun's side, dimmer sky opposite; a Belt of Venus pink band; clouds lit gold on the sun's side, rosy opposite, lavender undersides; per-world sunset cloud colours.
- **Grade**: saturation 1.08, contrast 1.12, split tone (shade towards teal-blue, light towards amber), stronger vignette.
- **Cost**, Apple M2, 1440×810 at 2x, meadow hole 6, in-play camera, GPU-synced: Low 2.1 ms, Medium 9.4, High ~19, Ultra ~40 (a different camera from the v1.8 numbers, so not directly comparable). The shaft pass alone: Medium ~1.0 ms, High ~1.8 ms, Ultra ~4–5 ms.
- Checked by eye: meadow, candy, frozen, clockwork, space, the Fog Bank omen, Low. Not verified: Windows GPUs and phones.

## v1.8.1 — graphics fixes (done)
- **Quality applies at once**: `CourseView.rebuildScenery(density, grass)` swaps the landscape and decor in place (the course, balls and items are untouched); `PlayScene.refreshGraphics()` and `MenuBackdrop.refreshGraphics()` call it from `Game.applySettings` when the tier changes. Scenery density and grass now live in `TIERS` (one table for play and the menu). No leaks: 82 geometries / 33 textures before and after seven switches.
- **Leaf edges**: the bright speckled rim round every branch (reported with a screenshot) came from alpha-to-coverage under the HDR post chain; with it off the rim was gone. Leaves now use a sharpened 0.5 alpha test, and canopies take only the texture's alpha (their colour is the palette), so nothing under the cut-out bleeds in. `build-nature.ts` also pads every texture's colour out under its transparent area (WebP `exact`), so ferns and flowers lose their dark fringes. Edges are smoothed by the new edge filters.
- **Anti-aliasing setting** (`settings.aa`): Auto (Low 2x MSAA; Medium 2x + FXAA; High and Ultra 4x + SMAA), Off, FXAA, MSAA 2x/4x/8x (capped at the GPU's `MAX_SAMPLES`), SMAA + MSAA 4x. FXAA/SMAA run after tone mapping, before the grain.
- **Grain flicker, root cause**: the composer ping-pongs two buffers and only `renderTarget2` is multisampled. With an odd number of swapping passes the scene was drawn into the plain buffer every other frame, so edges flickered between smooth and jagged. Turning grain on made the count odd on Medium and up; Low (one OutputPass) had flickered like this all along. `Renderer.render` now swaps back to the multisampled buffer before each frame (checked: 4 samples on every frame with an odd pass count). The grain also takes a fresh pattern every frame (it held each one for 2–3 frames, which strobed), and its top strength is about a third of v1.8.0 (0.055 vs 0.17).

## v1.8 — graphics overhaul (done)
- **Art direction**: classic cartoon battle-royale (bright saturated colours, chunky layered pines, puffy lime canopies, warm painted cliffs, hazy blue distance, big soft clouds, glassy blue water).
- **Scenery models**: Quaternius' Stylized Nature MegaKit (CC0, free Standard pack). `scripts/build-nature.ts` merges the 26 models the game uses into `client/src/assets/nature.glb` (1.7 MB) plus `rocks-cliff.webp`. Canopy leaves get baked soft "puffy" normals (out of the canopy: a sphere for round trees and bushes, a cone for pines) and a vertex colour holding the height gradient (R) and depth in the canopy (G). Leaf textures become white alpha masks, so each world's palette sets the colour. Normal maps are dropped, textures shrunk to 256–512 px WebP, meshes quantised and meshopt-compressed (decoded by three's `MeshoptDecoder`).
- **`rendering/nature.ts`**: loads the models at boot, in parallel with physics (~50 ms). Foliage is Lambert (no specular: puffy normals made canopy edges glow white under the standard material). The canopy shader turns the baked data into the world palette (meadow lime, candy pink/white, frozen snow tips, clockwork autumn, space glowing teal), adds wind sway and per-tree tint variation, and lights both sides of each leaf card from outside the canopy. Leaf alpha is boosted with the mip level and sharpened (distant trees stay solid with crisp edges, using alpha-to-coverage under MSAA). Rocks use the warm cliff paint (meadow, clockwork) or are recoloured from their brightness (pastel pink, icy blue, space purple). Geometry stays quantised; each part's matrix carries the decode transform. (Baking it into the 16-bit positions clamped every model into a ±1 m box, the first bug.)
- **Decor** (`decor.ts`): on the model tiers, scenery kinds map to models (tree, pine, snow pine, dead tree, bush, fern, flower, mushroom, rock, pebble), with per-world scatter lists. Candy keeps lollipops and gumdrops, Clockwork keeps gears and pipes, Frozen keeps snowmen and crystals. Tall trees are kept 3.2 m back from the rails so they never wall in the camera. Scenery beyond 7 m doesn't render into the shadow map (it covers only the course).
- **Landscape**: smooth-shaded terrain on the model tiers, model cliff buttes and spires instead of the faceted grey icosahedra, extra blue haze on the far mountain layers, warmer meadow rock.
- **Skies** (`rendering/sky.ts`): three-stop gradient, sun disc and wide halo, warm horizon glow on the sun side, domain-warped cumulus lit by sampling density towards the sun (lavender undersides, bright tops, silver lining), cirrus. Extras per world: a pastel rainbow (candy), aurora curtains (frozen), long layered golden-hour clouds and a lower sun (clockwork), nebulae, a galaxy band and twinkling shader stars (space; the old points starfield is gone). The sun direction is per world and drives the shadows. `uDetail` trims noise octaves per tier (3–6).
- **Water**: two drifting ripple layers, Schlick fresnel reflection of the world's own sky and sun, a deep-to-shallow body, painted crest bands and small round sun sparkles.
- **Film grain**: a final pass after tone mapping (display space, about one screen pixel, new noise 24 times a second, strongest in the midtones). Setting `grain` 0–100, default 0 (the pass is disabled at 0). Measured mean neighbouring-pixel difference: 2.2 off, 10.3 at 60, 18.7 at 100. `applySettings` now rebuilds the post stack only when the quality tier changes, not on every slider tick.
- **Presets**, measured GPU-synced on an Apple M2, 1440×810 at 2x, meadow hole 6. v1.7.2 → v1.8: Medium 11.1 → 12.6 ms, High 26.8 → 21.3 ms, Ultra 79.8 → 53.3 ms. GTAO cost ~12 ms on High and was nearly invisible under the stylised shading, so High drops it; Ultra keeps it at half resolution. Low keeps the classic procedural props (2 ms).
- Dev: `?backdrop=meadow:3&orbit=1.2` pins the menu backdrop to a hole and camera angle.
- Not verified: Windows GPUs, phones (Low/Medium on iOS), and the look of every Planet Hopper stage.

## v1.7.2 — ready-up fix, force start (done)
- **Ready-up on Windows 11, actually fixed**: reproduced in two Chromium tabs on Windows 11. A direct-game host attached its data-channel `onmessage` inside `onopen`, but Chromium can deliver the guest's first messages (its `hello` and `joinRoom`, sent the moment the guest's side opens) before the host's `open` event runs. They were dropped, so the host never identified the guest and answered every later message (Ready included) with `no_hello` "Say hello first". `LocalHost.createInvite` now creates the connection and handler up front and queues replies until the channel reports `open`. Belt and braces on the guest: a `no_hello` error makes the client say hello again and repeat its last `joinRoom` (`Game.sayHelloAgain`, throttled to once per 1.5 s; rejoining an existing seat resumes it).
- **Ready button**: flips at once and resends every 1.5 s until the host's room update confirms it; after four tries it says the host isn't getting it (ask them to force start, or rejoin). A second click while one is pending undoes it.
- **Force start** (host): next to the greyed-out Start button while someone isn't ready; asks first, then sends `start` with `force: true` (optional field, so older servers just answer `not_ready`). Players who didn't ready up still play; a system chat line says the host started without them.
- **Message limit** `NET.maxMessageBytes` 4 KB to 256 KB: `hello` and `profile` carry the whole profile, which the schema allows far past 4 KB (2000 collectibles, 500 cosmetics), and an oversized message closes the link.
- Tests: force start (host only, only when asked, the unready player plays), a lost hello recovered by saying it again, a profile over 4 KB.
- **Release**: 1.7.2 in `package.json`, `desktop/package.json`, the lockfile and the iOS `MARKETING_VERSION`. Windows build only for this release (built on Windows); the Mac build was not made.

## v1.7.1 — solid ledges, ping, intro rework, majority votes (done)
- **Wall the ball went through** (root cause): at a Planet Hopper **drop** the upper green is a 0.4 m floor slab, but the ledge is 0.7–1.1 m high. Below the slab the face is drawn only by the slab's side and its earth skirt (`CourseView.addSkirt`, visual only; nothing at all in Space), so there was a 0.3–0.7 m open slot between the slab and the lower green. A ball putted back towards the ledge rolled into the grey face, under the upper green, and fell out of bounds (26 of 32 drops over 10 seeds at 40% power). **Leap** ledges (spring pad and kicker) had the same slot above their water gap. Fix: `riser()` (builder) adds a solid floor-coloured block under every drop and leap ledge, from the slab down to the lower level, face 3 mm proud of the skirt so they don't z-fight (`generate.ts`). Affected: every generated hole with a drop or a leap, in every world. Hand-made courses have no such ledges, and `validate:courses` is unchanged. Tests: putting back at every drop over 12 seeds never gets under the slab, and horizontal rays at every leap face hit it (both fail on v1.7.0).
- **Bumps** (`shape: 'dome'`) collided as the whole sphere their cap is cut from (radius up to ~1.9 m, centre up to 1.7 m under the green): hidden under a solid green, but bulging out past rails and floor edges. They are now the convex hull of the visible cap (`domeCapPoints`), matching the mesh. Test: a ball dropping past a green's end beside a bump falls straight down.
- Audited and consistent: obstacle parts and static solids (shared by physics and renderer), tunnels and decor (visual only, never in play), occlusion fading (fades rails/terraces between camera and ball but changes no physics).
- **Slick Greens**: `SLICK_GREEN_GRIP` 1.2 → 1.44 (another 20% of rolling resistance and drag on slick, frost and rime). The test putt rolls 26.5 m instead of 31.8 m (true ice: 38.2 m), so ~30% less far than ice; still slick < frost < rime < grass.
- **Quit to desktop**: *⏻ Quit to desktop* in the main menu's bottom-right corner, desktop app only (a browser tab can't close itself and iOS apps don't quit themselves). The preload exposes `pppNative.quit()`, which invokes `app:quit` in the main process: a full `app.quit()` on every platform, macOS included; `before-quit` still stops a LAN server.
- **Ping**: the Tab scorecard has a *Ping* column online (`42 ms`, coloured good/fair/poor, *reconnecting…* for dropped players; relay teams list players under the table), redrawn every 0.5 s while open. Your own ping comes from your live RTT (every 2 s), others' from the room state (every ≤3 s). **Show ping** (Settings → Graphics, next to Show FPS; off by default, saved with the other settings) shows your ping beside the FPS counter, online only. No protocol change: `RoomPlayer.ping` already existed.
- **Hole intro** (`HoleIntro.ts`): 2 s on the cup with the name and par card, then one eased pass (smootherstep) back along the whole centreline to the tee, then a 1.2 s ease into the gameplay camera: 6 s for the shortest hole up to 14 s (`2 + clamp(2.2 + 0.16·length, 2.8, 10.8) + 1.2`); it waits while the game is paused. The camera rides over the course's own centreline a few metres behind the spot shown and looks ahead along it, so every leg, fork lane and set piece passes through the frame and it never swings out over the trees. Its height follows a smoothed profile that stays 1.6 m above every solid within 1.4 m (towers, overpasses, windmill roofs, loops). Shots stay locked until it ends or is skipped (any key or tap, 0.45 s glide). It is still local-only, and no turn timer can run out during it: matches have no shot clock, and the only countdown (Planet Hopper votes) runs between holes, before the next hole and its intro start. **Rest stops** have no intro, card or lock (`introPlan`, from the run's fixed skeleton so it doesn't depend on a late score online); boss and normal stages keep it.
- **Majority vote** (`HopperMatch`): with more than two connected players, a strict majority of the connected players on one option cuts the countdown to `MAJORITY_SECONDS = 5` (only ever down). Decisions: the cut deadline sticks if a changed vote breaks the majority up, and votes can change until it runs out; only connected players count, for the majority and the final tally; a disconnect re-checks (all remaining voted → decided at once; a new majority → cut); fewer than three connected never cuts; a reroll deals fresh options with fresh votes and a fresh 25 s. The current majority rides on `choice.majority` (optional, so older peers ignore it); the vote card shows *Majority reached, deciding in Ns* from the host's countdown. Tests: 2 players, 3 with two matching votes, 4 split 2–2, a changed vote, disconnects, a reroll, and the wire format with and without the field.
- **Release**: 1.7.1 in `package.json`, `desktop/package.json`, the lockfile and the iOS project's `MARKETING_VERSION` (it had been left at 1.2.0).

## v1.7 — invite codes, fixes, hole intros, scenery, kick (done)
- **Shorter invite codes** (`client/src/net/compactCode.ts`): a data-channel SDP is nearly all boilerplate, so only the ICE ufrag/pwd, the DTLS fingerprint, the role and the candidate addresses (IPv4, up to two global IPv6, mDNS names, public addresses) are packed into bytes, checksummed (CRC-16) and written in Base58 behind a `PPP2` prefix: ~190 characters instead of ~700, letters and digits only. Anything that doesn't fit (more than 15 addresses, an unusual fingerprint) falls back to the old `PPP1.` format, which is still read. The code is found inside a pasted chat message. Tests round-trip a realistic Windows 11 candidate set.
- **Ready-up fix** (a guest on Windows 11 couldn't ready up; not reproducible here, so this is the set of faults found by reading the path end to end). The host handles `ready` correctly, and the short codes are not involved (the report is against v1.6.0, which doesn't have them; once the link is open the code plays no part). What was wrong on the guest's side: (1) the guest lobby is ~860 px tall and the Ready button was its last element, so on a short window (a 1080p laptop at 150% scaling gives ~640 px) it was entirely below the fold; it is now pinned to the bottom of the window. (2) The lobby was rebuilt from scratch on every room update (at least every 3 s), which dropped any click that straddled a rebuild and closed the host's course menu; it now only updates pings unless something changed. (3) The Ready button decided from the room state it was drawn with, so a double-click could send ready then un-ready; it now reads the live state and ignores a second click within 0.4 s. (4) `NetClient` let a replaced transport's late `close` event mark the new link offline, stop its pings and (direct games) throw the player back to the menu; stale transports are now ignored. (5) Pasting a second invite left the first attempt's peer connection knocking for 15 minutes, and whichever link opened last took over; a new attempt now cancels the previous one. (6) `Room.broadcast` stopped at the first connection whose `send` threw; each send is now guarded.
- **Loop-the-loop invisible**: `CourseView` merges low floors into one static mesh per material and flushed that batch *before* adding obstacles, so the loop's own floor segments (role `floor`) were queued and never drawn. Raised segments outside Space escaped because raised floors are separate meshes, hence "sometimes". Obstacle floors are now always separate meshes (they also fade when they hide the ball).
- **Slick Greens on slopes**: with a Storm Compass at Lv2 the omen skipped every sloped floor (physics and look), which read as slopes not freezing, most often on Space, the last sector, where the compass has usually been levelled. The omen now covers every floor at every level: `slick` (no compass), `frost` (Lv1), `rime` (Lv2, a thin frost, rolling 0.62 × grip), nothing at Lv3. **Slide tuning**: `SLICK_GREEN_GRIP = 1.2` in `shared/src/config.ts` scales rolling resistance and drag of those three surfaces; a measured putt rolls ~17% less far than on ice. Ice patches and every other surface are unchanged.
- **Hole intro** (`client/src/rendering/HoleIntro.ts`): a 3–5 s flyover (length scales with the hole) along a Catmull-Rom curve through `hole.path`, tee to cup, then back along the hole, blending into the gameplay camera over the last stretch so the final frame is exactly the gameplay view. A card shows the hole's name and par. Any key or tap skips it (a 0.45 s glide, never a cut). It is presentation only: the match, turns and timers run as before, every client plays it when it loads the hole, and input is only held back locally while it runs. Reduced-motion shows the card without the camera move; `?nointro` turns it off.
- **Terrain** (`Landscape.ts`): beyond 16 m from the course each sector gets its own landforms on top of the old hills: meadow downs and a grass ridge, candy gumdrop mounds and sugar dunes, frozen stepped ice shelves and a knife ridge, clockwork mesas over a dune floor, space craters with raised rims, regolith dunes and a rim ridge. Strength, direction and palette shift per hole (seeded). Two unlit silhouette rings of far mountains (two draw calls) add depth; steep faces get strata bands. The near field is untouched, so nothing rises into the course or the camera's line.
- **Kick**: `kick{id}` (client → host) and `kicked{message}` (host → client). `Room.kick` checks on the host that the sender is the host and the target isn't; the player is removed from the room and from relay turns, told why, and (server rooms) can't rejoin with the code. Direct hosts close the link and can re-invite. UI: **✕ Remove** on other players' lobby cards and *Pause → Players*, host only, with a confirmation.
- **Version**: the root `package.json` is the single source; Vite injects it (`__APP_VERSION__`), the main menu shows it bottom-left, and a test fails if `desktop/package.json` or the lockfile disagree.

## v1.6 — polish and bug fixes (done)
- **Seamless smooth floors**: every curve (`arc`), slide and corkscrew is one ribbon whose ends are cut square to the straight floor they meet (`ribbonEdges(path, w, dir0, dir1)`, `fairway({ smooth })` stops neighbouring boxes from overlapping into them). Ribbons can carry explicit edge lines (`Solid.left/right`, `strip()`). Fork swoops are a shared strip that splits exactly at the island tip into two lane strips; every cut is square to the leg (lane half-width `W / 2 / cos(slope)`), so pieces meet edge to edge. Before, overlapping boxes z-fought and left wedge-shaped pinholes on S-curves, worst on fork lanes. Slides are now an S that leaves and rejoins the leg tangentially. Test: smooth floors never overlap another floor.
- **Tunnels** skip bends tighter than the tube (it folded over itself on the inside) and anything within reach of another part of the course, above, below or alongside; slides get a wider tube so their taller rails stay inside.
- **Grass**: scenery used to be kept out of every solid's axis-aligned box, which cleared big squares round diagonal legs, curves and corkscrews. `client/src/rendering/footprint.ts` rasterises the exact footprint into a 0.2 m grid with a distance field: grass grows to the rails, and placement is O(1) per blade.
- **Slick Greens** with a Storm Compass turned the green to `metal`, barely different from grass; it now becomes `frost` (new surface, rolling 0.36) at Lv1, frost on the flats only at Lv2.
- **Gimme Clover** added a stroke, so it never saved one; the gimme is now free, reach 0.9 / 1.3 / 1.7 m. **Spring pads** didn't launch a ball that arrived in the air (the "already used" flag was set on entering the zone, not on launching); fixed and tested. Pads now bounce for every launch (on the `boost` event) rather than whenever a ball is near.
- **Difficulty**: measured par against the solver per stage (2 × 3 seeds, old and new generator): the margin grew from ~1.1 strokes early to ~2 late, so late holes were relatively easy, and a few long downhill holes were giveaways (par 7, solved in 2). Par is now the raw estimate tightened by `0.8·t`, clamped to 0.5–1.8 strokes above a solver-stroke estimate fitted on ~180 generated holes (`1.07 + 0.025·flat m + 0.03·downhill m + 0.12·sharp corners − 0.05·curves + 0.167·hazard points`, rms 0.8). Measured margins over the solver by quarter of the run: 0.83 / 1.38 / 1.71 / 1.61 (v1.5: 1.09 / 1.76 / 2.03 / 1.81). Holes have up to 8 legs and set pieces are commoner and earlier. Heart pools 3 / 6 / 12 / 16 / 22 / 27. The run model (v1.5 skill model shifted by the measured margins) gives an average solo player ~35%, duo ~34%, trio 36–51% (mixed / average skill), four ~41%; it is a model, not playtesting. Rare trinkets ×0.25 draft weight (was ×0.5), Mystery Box rare chance 40% (was 60%).
- **Variety**: Rolling Hills (bumps, humps, dips) and Thrill Ride (late rollercoasters; replaces Classic from depth 10) personalities, deck of 11 with step 4; double corkscrews (depth ≥ 9), steep chutes (2–2.7 m drop), **bumps** (`shape: 'dome'`, a spherical cap 9–18 cm high on one side of the green).
- **Camera** passes through rails and obstacles instead of lurching in; whatever stands between it and the ball (rails, blocks, machinery, raised terraces) fades to ~28% (`CourseView.updateOcclusion`).
- **Putters**: 12 styles with distinct heads (blade, mallet, twin fang, hockey stick, candy-cane crook, rocket, star wand, flamingo, croquet mallet…), two more free; looks only. **Putter sound**: a CC0 recording (inlined), pitched and levelled by power, with the old synth as fallback.
- **Performance**: low floors and earth skirts merge into one mesh per material; every rail shares one wood material (plank repeat baked into UVs); ice/frost materials are cached per hole instead of one canvas texture per floor.
- Fix: ribbon floors set `DoubleSide` on the shared cached floor-side material.

## Decisions (simplest option that keeps game feel)

- **Ball physics:** rotation-locked sphere, zero friction; rolling resistance/drag/settling are our own code (`config.PHYSICS`, `SURFACES`). Much easier to tune than Rapier friction on a sphere.
- **Cup:** detection-based (no hole in the collider). Lip-out = deflection + speed loss when crossing faster than `sinkSpeed`.
- **Surfaces as zones**, not collider patches, to avoid seam bumps.
- **JSON store instead of SQLite** (native addon friction on Node 26) — see ARCHITECTURE.md.
- **Courses are TS data validated by zod** at load (and in tests), written with builder helpers (`fairway()` builds floors, ramps, mitred walls).
- Daily challenge is played through the server (authoritative leaderboard) and falls back to local play offline.

## Tuning notes

- `maxShotSpeed 17`, `powerCurve 1.35` (fine control on short putts), grass `rolling 1.15 / drag 0.32` → 50% power ≈ 9 units.
- `sinkSpeed 4.4`: a firm putt still drops; full-power shots lip out.

## Known issues (v1.1)
- Desktop/iOS builds are unsigned/ad-hoc signed: first launch shows OS warnings (see README). iOS device installs need the user's Apple team selected in Xcode.
- No iOS Simulator runtime was installed on the build machine, so the iPhone app was compiled but not run in a simulator.
- Direct play can fail on symmetric NATs (no TURN relay, by design: no servers).
- v1.1.1 fix: direct play between different home networks failed whenever the host took more than ~15 s to paste the reply code (the guest's browser abandons ICE checks after 15 s of silence, and the host's router drops the guest's packets until the host starts sending). Candidates are now stripped from the codes' SDP and re-added with fresh foundations whenever checks give up (`keepKnocking` in `p2p.ts`), so both sides keep punching until connected. Code generation also stops ~1.2 s after the public address is found instead of waiting out the 4 s gathering timeout.

## Known issues

- Guest profiles are client-held and pushed on `hello`; a tampered localStorage profile can be merged into the server copy. Acceptable for guest-only play; with real auth the server should stop accepting client progress.
- Rooms live in memory: a server restart (including `tsx watch` reloads in dev) closes all rooms.
- In a hidden browser tab rendering pauses, so remote events queue until the tab is visible again (then flush).
- Not verified by a human playtest at full frame rate: development checks ran in an embedded browser pane that was hidden (throttled to ~2 fps), so animation smoothness was verified by tests/metrics rather than by eye.
- Rapier's `-compat` build inlines its WASM (1.67 MB gzipped); the boot splash covers the download.
- Pars are designer estimates; the greedy solver is superhuman (it aces many holes), so it only proves completability, not difficulty.
